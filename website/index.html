The payload

The question it actually answers

AlphaFold reports a per-residue confidence (pLDDT). The genuinely useful thing to know is where the model was confident but wrong — high pLDDT, yet far from the real structure. OpenFoldUI correlates confidence against measured error (Spearman ρ), surfaces the exact residues that break the trend, and paints them onto a 3D structure: blue = good, red = confidently wrong.

That single check — is the confidence honest? — is what turns a prediction into something you can responsibly build on.

What it does

A whole companion workflow

From a single prediction to a labelled dataset — every step runs locally.

Inspect

Any AlphaFold model on its own — pLDDT confidence, predicted disordered regions, PAE, and domains — for any of 200M+ proteins, no experimental structure required.

Compare

Kabsch superposition against the best experimental structure: RMSD, TM-score, GDT-TS, lDDT, per-residue deviation, and the pLDDT-vs-error calibration plot.

Fold

Fold a raw sequence with ESMFold (or your own AlphaFold/ColabFold endpoint) through a persistent queue, then feed it straight into a comparison.

Multi-chain & assemblies

Compare a monomer model against every experimental chain of an oligomer, and measure deviation at the assembly interface versus the core.

Predictor face-off

Fold the same sequence with ESMFold and benchmark AlphaFold vs ESMFold vs experiment, metric by metric, with a per-metric winner.

Dataset analytics

Score a whole list of accessions at once: aggregate pLDDT distribution, disorder fractions, a ranked table, and CSV export.

Conformational landscapes

Compare against multiple experimental states — apo/holo, open/closed — and see which conformation the model predicted and how flexible the protein is.

Guided tour

An interactive education mode that runs a live p53 comparison and teaches every metric on real, freshly-computed numbers.

A real workspace

IndexedDB history with favorites, tags, projects, notes; a dashboard; Excel/CSV/JSON export, paper bundles, and shareable permalinks.

Under the hood

How a comparison is built

A pure, headless TypeScript engine — no DOM, no network, fully unit-tested — at the core.

  1. 1

    Parse

    Read the AlphaFold .pdb and the experimental structure (PDBe updated mmCIF, with per-atom UniProt numbers).

  2. 2

    Number

    Align both to a common UniProt numbering via SIFTS — the right way, so residues match exactly.

  3. 3

    Align

    Inner-join the prediction and experiment on UniProt number into matched Cα coordinate arrays.

  4. 4

    Compare

    Kabsch superposition (SVD), then RMSD, TM-score, GDT-TS, lDDT, per-residue deviation, and Spearman(pLDDT, error).

Validated against canonical TM-align (WASM) to ~0.001 TM-score, and on real data (p53/2OCJ → RMSD 0.51 Å, TM 0.99, GDT 0.99).

Reference

Every metric, computed from scratch

MetricRangeWhat it measures
RMSD0 → ∞ ÅAverage Cα distance after best-fit superposition. Lower is better.
TM-score0 → 1Length-normalised fold similarity. ≥ 0.5 means the same fold.
GDT-TS0 → 1Mean fraction of residues within 1, 2, 4, 8 Å cutoffs.
lDDT0 → 1Local distance differences — superposition-free. Exactly what pLDDT predicts.
pLDDT–error ρ−1 → 1Spearman of confidence vs deviation. Negative = well-calibrated.

It all runs on your machine

Every metric is computed in your browser by a native TypeScript engine. Structures are fetched directly from public databases (AlphaFold DB, PDBe, UniProt); your own uploads never leave the page. The whole thing is an installable, offline-capable PWA — no server, no tracking, no sign-in.
